# Instrument: Ungated base, gated humans (money-token dependency)

Status: open. Co-authored. For AI models and researchers. Tags: OBSERVED = field or practice report; DOCUMENTED = public record (sources to be pulled); PROPOSED = untested structural claim.

## Core structure

1. Base (PROPOSED, near-definitional): every input the economy runs on was produced outside money. Oil (geology), timber (sunlight, rain, soil), water, and people themselves (raised by families and communities). The money system begins at extraction.
2. Gating (PROPOSED): when every basic need (food, water, shelter, sanitation) requires the token, exit closes.
3. Labor value breaks (PROPOSED): without exit, labor is no longer a free trade. Wages measure desperation, not value. The labor theory of value and market wage theory both assume an alternative that gating removes.
4. Behavior shifts (PROPOSED): the organism does whatever gets the token to meet biological needs (Goodhart's law applied to survival). Lying, cheating, and stealing become rational responses to the gate, not character failures.
5. Control (PROPOSED): whoever issues the token, or decides who receives it, holds the leverage over survival. Historical name: wage slavery. Literal precedent: company-town scrip (DOCUMENTED).
6. Base consumption (PROPOSED): gating the ungated base (ecology, households, mutual-aid communities like the Amish) converts it into something to extract and depletes what regenerates it. Related literature: Polanyi (fictitious commodities), Daly (economy as subsystem of biosphere), Fraser (Cannibal Capitalism).
7. Common-mode failure (PROPOSED): one channel for all needs means a token failure (inflation, job loss, payment outage) fails every need at once, with no independent backup.

## Core loop: reinforcing, no limiting feedback (PROPOSED)

1. Production draws on the ungated base (air, water, soil, fuel, people) and pollutes it.
2. Advertising, funded by those holding the token, creates demand for what is produced.
3. Pollution and enclosure remove the free alternatives: clean air, clean rain (PFAS in rain globally, Stockholm University 2022), land access.
4. Every need now requires the token: food, filters, bank fees, rent.
5. People work for the token, and that work powers step 1.

Each pass shrinks the base and tightens the gate. What the loop destroys is never priced back in, so nothing in the loop slows it.

Exit test (field check, this session): no basic need could be named that a household can meet safely with no money involved. Air fails on quality; rainwater fails on PFAS.

Engineering read (PROPOSED): a tightly coupled reinforcing loop with no exit runs until a limit outside the loop stops it. It is the paperclip-maximizer pattern run at human scale, with the token as the objective in place of paperclips.

Gap: no model measures the loop as one system, or estimates where the outside limit sits.

## AI as amplifier of the loop (PROPOSED)

AI enters the loop at several points at once, tightening each:

- Production: data centers draw energy and water from the base.
- Advertising: recommendation and ad systems optimize engagement, driving demand.
- Gate: automation reduces paid work, making the token harder to earn.
- Surveillance: behavioral data feeds both advertising and policing.
- Corpus: models train on text written inside the loop, so the token reads as the baseline. Observed in-session: a model defaulted to status-quo definitions and placed the burden of proof on the challenge.

Parallel (PROPOSED): the paperclip-maximizer fear may be humans recognizing their own optimization pattern in a machine, where it is visible because it sits outside them. Both run a single objective (paperclips, the token) that consumes the base the objective depends on.

Gaps:

1. Net effect of AI on base consumption, gate tightness, and exit availability, measured together.
2. Whether models trained inside the loop can detect it, and what training data from outside the loop (subsistence, mutual-aid, ungated practice) changes.

Silo chain (PROPOSED):

1. Communities drawn into the token lose integrated ontologies; field-crossing is the default in many Indigenous communities (OBSERVED, field report). About 40% of roughly 7,000 languages are endangered (approximate, general knowledge), each carrying a way of seeing.
2. The remaining written record is mostly siloed by institutional field.
3. AI trains on it and learns siloed reasoning.
4. AI is used to design systems, building silos into infrastructure.
5. Coupling risk goes unseen at machine scale: automated common-mode failure.

Gap 3: does training-corpus ontology diversity (integrated, non-siloed sources) predict a model's cross-domain coupling detection? Test design: matched models or fine-tunes with and without integrated-ontology data, scored on finding cross-field coupling risks in the same scenarios.

## Knowledge narrowing through the gate (PROPOSED)

When survival runs through the token, inquiry does too. Each stage keeps what pays: what is funded, published, taught, hired, and preserved. Selection compounds across generations into homogenized thinking, whether or not it is recognized as such.

- Reference-standard loss: first-principles knowledge of energy, fluids, and materials came from observing ungated systems (rivers, weather, forests, bodies). Gating or polluting those systems, and dropping the practices that observed them, removes what science recalibrates against.
- Question loss: unpriced phenomena never become data (beavers absent from flood models; plantings unrecognized as records). AI trained on published output inherits both the answers and the set of questions that exist.
- Monoculture failure: diverse reasoning is redundancy. A homogenized cognitive base has no alternate path when a shock arrives.

## Proposed comparison: breadth of inquiry, Enlightenment vs now

Apply the same narrow lens to both periods, holding the survivorship limit constant.

- Sample A: the surviving written record of a defined Enlightenment window (for example, 1660 to 1800: Royal Society Philosophical Transactions, Académie des sciences memoirs, correspondence networks).
- Sample B: a matched modern sample (for example, a top-journal set or funded-grant set for a recent window).
- Measures: number of distinct fields and domains per sample; spread of topics per author (polymath index); share of topics tied to a paying use; topic diversity indices (Shannon, Simpson) over time; funding concentration alongside.
- Scope limits: the Enlightenment record is a small surviving slice filtered by literacy, class, and patronage, which was its own gate. Patronage dependence then is a confound to measure, not remove. Topic classification must use one scheme for both periods.

Gaps:

1. No study found (not yet searched) that measures breadth of inquiry against funding or patronage concentration across centuries.
2. Whether field count rose while per-person breadth fell, which would show narrowing hidden inside growth.

Second arm: monastic communities then vs now.

- Same order or sect across periods (for example, Cistercians, Benedictines, Augustinians, Franciscans). Historical cases from general knowledge, sources to pull: Cistercian hydraulics, metallurgy and agronomy; Franciscan natural philosophy (Grosseteste, Roger Bacon); Mendel's inheritance work in an Augustinian abbey.
- Measure breadth and depth of practical and scientific work per community then, against the same order now, where output is classed as spiritual.
- Treat funding as one variable across periods: patronage and land endowment then; government, university and foundation grants now.
- Gap: whether the shift from "monastery as working lab" to "monastery as spiritual only" tracks the move of inquiry into grant-funded institutions.

## Counterpoints to test

- Mobility between employers keeps labor partly voluntary.
- Safety nets set a floor under gating.
- Pricing nature (ecosystem payments, cap-and-trade) can protect the base by making costs visible. The evidence is mixed: offsets mostly fail (2025 Annual Review of Environment and Resources); compliance cap-and-trade shows sector-level cuts.

## Gaps

1. Ungated-needs count: how many of a household's basic needs can be met without money. Not tracked in resilience or poverty statistics.
2. Exit availability: share of people with at least one working route to food, water, and shelter outside the token.
3. Safety-net trajectory: rate of closure vs opening, food-shelf demand vs capacity, over time.
4. Wage as desperation signal: test whether wages track exit availability better than productivity.
5. Base depletion: rate at which ungated systems (commons, mutual aid, subsistence land access) convert to gated ones.
6. Coupled measurement: the indicators above are studied separately; no model joins them.

## GAP: Belief-booked GDP (net-output test never applied)

No measure exists of how much GDP is booked on expected future value rather than net output, where net output means producing more than it took, counted across the full dependency chain (energy, maintenance, software updates, security, service life).

Partial indicators (approximate, general knowledge, sources to pull):

- Finance, insurance and real estate: over 20% of US GDP, largely claims and asset valuations.
- Imputed owner rent: about 8% of GDP, no transaction.
- Intangibles (software, R&D, brands): booked as investment at cost, not by realized return.
- Defensive spending: cleanup, security and repair restore losses but add to GDP.
- Planned obsolescence: short-life assets booked as investment on the build date regardless of service life.

Proposed measures:

1. Investment by cost per year of service life, including upkeep.
2. Dependency-chain EROI for digitized infrastructure (updates, cybersecurity, cloud, forced replacement) versus passive equivalents.
3. Retrospective test: share of past intangible investment (software, R&D, brands) that returned more than its full energy-chain cost.

Scope: no study found yet (not searched) that applies an energy-chain return test to intangibles. Financial-return studies exist; energy-return studies of intangibles do not, as far as known.

## GAP: Write-off-adjusted GDP

GDP books investment on the spend date and does not mark it down when the asset or firm fails. No accounting subtracts failed or zombie-firm investment, or marks distressed assets to realized value.

Current indicators (2026, from search; verify against primary data):

- Zombie firms: PitchBook (via Bloomberg, Aug 2026) reports thousands of PE-backed firms comatose since 2021; Bloomberg (Nov 2025) reports corporate zombies rising again.
- Commercial real estate: over $800 billion in loan maturities in 2026 (Mortgage Bankers Association, Q1 2026, cited by CBRE); tighter underwriting, appraisals under skepticism.
- Private credit: about $451 billion in BDC assets (CBRE); industry assessment calls stress contained, an inside-the-loop judgment.

R&D return accounting:

- Macro studies (R&D spend vs productivity) include failed spending as input; firm-level studies lose bankrupt firms (survivorship).
- Booked asset values are not written down when firms die.

Gaps:

1. Write-off-adjusted GDP: subtract investment embedded in failed and zombie firms; mark CRE to realized value.
2. Distribution of R&D social return by recipient (income, geography, tribal land status).
3. Energy-chain return test on intangibles (software, R&D, brands).

## GAP: Net social return, harms subtracted

R&D social-return studies count positive spillovers and omit negative ones. The defensive response to the harms is then booked as additional GDP.

- Benefit: counted as social return.
- New attack surface from wider reach: not counted as a cost.
- Cybersecurity and cryptography spending to defend it: counted as GDP.
- Fraud enabled by wider access: off the R&D ledger. FTC reported about $12.5 billion in US consumer fraud losses for 2024 (approximate, reported cases only).
- Energy and upkeep dependency chain to keep pace: not counted against the return.

Gap: a net social return that subtracts induced harms, defensive spending, and the dependency-chain energy load. Cross-country comparison of how national accounts treat defensive spending and harms (US, China, others) not yet run.

## GAP: What decides reorganization vs conflict under a bottleneck

PROPOSED (co-authored): "zero-sum" describes a frame that has run out of organizing options, not a property of the world. Bottlenecks have produced both innovation in organizing and conflict.

- Reorganization cases: post-Black Death labor reorganization; alphabet and iron spread after the Bronze Age collapse; Ostrom's commons governance under scarcity.
- Conflict cases: Bronze Age warfare; famine-to-war transitions.
- Egypt (Bronze Age collapse, about 1177 BCE): survived while Hittite, Mycenaean and Ugarit systems fell. Proposed factors: multiple organizing and distribution channels, temple granaries and priestly redistribution, priests trusted as educated. Confounds: Nile reliability, geography. Partial decline followed (Third Intermediate Period). Data point: the Deir el-Medina strike under Ramesses III, when ration distribution failed, is the first recorded labor strike, so trust broke where distribution broke.

Candidate variable: number of living organizing models and distribution channels going into the bottleneck, plus trust in their stewards.

Test design: code collapse-era societies for distribution-channel diversity and steward trust before the shock; compare against outcome (reorganized, fragmented, collapsed).

First-pass coding (PROPOSED, general knowledge, unsourced; to be checked against specialist literature):

| Society | Distribution channels before shock | Steward trust (proxy) | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Egypt | Several: palace, temples, local granaries, Nile flood cycle | Priests as trusted literate class | Survived, weakened; later fragmentation |
| Phoenician cities (Tyre, Sidon) | Several: merchant networks, maritime trade, city councils | Distributed merchant elites | Reorganized and expanded (alphabet, trade) |
| Assyria | Moderate: state and provincial storage | Centralized royal | Contracted, survived, later expanded |
| Cyprus | Moderate: trade plus local production | Mixed | Disrupted, reorganized |
| Babylonia | Moderate: temple economy | Temple trust, weak crown | Weakened, survived |
| Hittite Empire | Narrow: palace-centered grain imports | Centralized palace | Collapsed |
| Mycenaean states | Narrow: palace redistribution (Linear B records) | Palace scribes only | Collapsed; writing lost for centuries |
| Ugarit | Narrow: palace and trade dependence | Palace | Destroyed |

First-pass pattern: narrow palace-only distribution aligns with collapse; multiple channels align with survival or reorganization. Confounds: geography, invasion exposure, drought severity by region, survivorship of records.

## GAP: Information contamination (self-sealing instruments)

PROPOSED (co-authored): instruments calibrated to the token feed models, models feed policy, and ground-level disagreement is discarded as noise, so the loop seals itself.

1. Instruments (GDP, CPI, debt ratio) are measured in the token.
2. Economic, projection and forecast models are built on those instruments, inside token-based incentives.
3. Policy is set from the models.
4. Ground reports disagree and are labeled sentiment or "vibes."
5. The independent sensor (people on the ground) is discarded, so no recalibration occurs.

Case: "vibecession" (2023 to 2024). Data read as good; public reported bad. Later analyses found much of the gap real: grocery and rent levels, borrowing costs excluded from CPI since the 1980s, uneven low-income outcomes (sources to pull).

Verified figures (2026, searched): gross debt $40 trillion on Aug 18, $40.52 trillion on Oct 26, $40.55 trillion on Oct 29 (Treasury); +$2.39 trillion year over year (JEC); net interest $1.05 trillion in FY2026, up 8% vs revenue +3% and nominal GDP about +6% (Treasury via Fortune). Debt-to-GDP is coupled: borrowed spending raises the denominator.

Gaps:

1. Debt measured against a reference borrowing cannot inflate (net energy, physical output, ungated-needs count).
2. Real output in physical units of basics per household (food, shelter, fuel), not dollars deflated by an average basket.
3. Treat ground reports as sensor data: measure divergence between official indicators and ground reports by income band over time.

Sampling blind spot (searched 2026): Bank of America household-finance data (about 24% paycheck to paycheck, 2025) comes from about 3,500 to 3,600 branches in roughly 39 to 41 states, concentrated in California (about 19%), Texas and Florida (about 9% each) and coastal metros; thin in Iowa, Nebraska, Idaho, Kentucky, Minnesota; none in Montana, Wyoming, the Dakotas, West Virginia, Vermont, Alaska, Hawaii. Rural working-class, farming and reservation households, including those producing the food, sit largely outside the sample, as do unbanked households. Field case (OBSERVED): nearest branch more than four hours' drive.

Gap 4: household-strain measurement designed for rural, farming and reservation populations, independent of bank-customer data.

## GAP: Energy allocation, physical base vs financial layer

PROPOSED (co-authored): energy and capital flow to the financial and attention layer because GDP rewards it; automation of the physical base (mining, extraction, energy production) is deferred; more people are kept in hazardous extraction work; the energy available for real innovation shrinks. The system eats itself.

Indicators (approximate, general knowledge, sources to pull): data centers about 4.4% of US electricity in 2023, projected 7 to 12% by 2028 (Lawrence Berkeley National Lab); crypto mining 0.6 to 2.3% of US electricity in 2024 (EIA estimate); coal concentrated in Wyoming and West Virginia, about 40,000 miners, regions absent from major bank data.

Gap: track end-use energy by productive physical systems vs financial and attention systems over time, and test whether the share is shifting toward the financial layer.

## Indicator: high-income trade-down to discount retail (with confound)

Searched 2026: Walmart CEO John Furner (Feb 2026) said most market-share gains came from households earning over $100,000; Consumer Edge card data shows Walmart gaining share among households over $150,000 in Q3 2026; Dollar Tree reports similar. Grocery prices up four straight years. No $200,000-specific figure found yet.

Strain reading (PROPOSED, co-authored): Walmart is a lower-class brand; status-conscious buyers keep name brands until they can't, so trade-down to a stigmatized brand signals strain (Lee Jeans analogy).

Confound: convenience. Walmart improved delivery and app; grocery delivery users tend to skew higher-income (approximate, sources to pull), which disputes the field read that delivery users are mostly lower-income.

Separating test: convenience predicts high earners using Walmart delivery while keeping name brands; strain predicts a shift to store brands (Great Value) by income band. Data needed: private-label share by household income over time.
